# Analysis Dimensions: Vocabulary and Taxonomies

Use this when you need the precise term for something you can see. Correct terminology is what makes an analysis reproducible. "Soft side light" is weaker than "large soft key at 45 degrees camera-left, short side lit, with a subtle rim from behind." Reach for the specific name.

## 1. Medium and genre

**Mediums**: photograph, digital 3D render (octane or unreal look), 2D illustration, vector, painterly digital, oil or gouache, watercolor, ink, paper-cut or layered paper, collage, claymation or stop-motion frame, knitted or felted, pixel art, AI-generated composite, photobash, mixed media.

**Photographic genres**: high-fashion editorial, beauty, portrait (studio, environmental, documentary), street, fashion lookbook, product or still life, food, architecture or interior, landscape, conceptual or fine art, lifestyle or UGC, advertising or campaign.

Name the medium first. A paper-cut look and a 3D render of the same subject need completely different prompts.

## 2. Composition and framing

**Framing systems**: rule of thirds, centered or symmetrical, golden ratio or spiral, dynamic diagonal, frame-within-a-frame.

**Shot scale**: extreme close-up, close-up, medium close-up, medium, medium-full, full body, wide, extreme wide.

**Balance**: symmetrical, asymmetrical, radial, weighted to one side with counterbalancing negative space.

**Other tools**: leading lines, negative space, headroom and lead room, foreground framing, layering and depth, repetition or pattern, horizon placement.

## 3. Camera and shot type

**Angle**: eye-level, high angle, low angle, overhead or top-down (flat lay), worm's-eye, dutch or canted tilt.

**Distance and compression**: a wide focal length up close exaggerates perspective and feels intimate or distorted. A long focal length compresses and flattens, which reads as premium and editorial.

**Focal-length character** (describe the look, since a generator infers from it): wide environmental (24mm to 35mm look), natural (50mm look), portrait compression (85mm to 135mm look), macro.

## 4. Lens and optics

**Depth of field**: deep (everything sharp), moderate, shallow (subject sharp, background soft), razor-thin.

**Bokeh**: smooth and creamy, busy or nervous, swirly, cat-eye toward edges, hexagonal or octagonal specular highlights.

**Optical signatures**: lens flare, veiling glare, chromatic aberration at edges, barrel or pincushion distortion, vignetting, anamorphic horizontal flares and oval bokeh.

## 5. Lighting patterns and quality

**Portrait patterns**: flat or broad, loop, Rembrandt (triangle of light on the shadow cheek), split, butterfly or paramount (beauty light from above), short lit, broad lit, clamshell (key above plus fill below).

**Source quality**: hard (small or distant source, crisp shadows) vs soft (large or diffused source, gradual shadows).

**Roles**: key (main), fill (lifts shadows, sets contrast ratio), rim or back or kicker (separates subject from background), background or set light, practicals (lamps visible in frame), bounce.

**Direction**: front, side, three-quarter, back or contre-jour, top, under.

**Natural conditions**: golden hour, blue hour, harsh midday, open shade, overcast or diffused, window light, dappled light through foliage.

**Studio signatures**: single dramatic strobe with deep falloff, beauty dish, softbox, octabox, ring light (round catchlights), gridded spot, gelled color.

**Contrast ratio**: low-key (dark, dramatic, high contrast), high-key (bright, airy, low contrast), balanced.

## 6. Color and grade

**Saturation**: muted or desaturated, natural, rich, hyper-saturated, selectively saturated.

**Grades and looks**: teal and orange, bleach bypass (desaturated high contrast), crushed blacks, lifted or faded blacks (matte film look), split-toning (warm highlights, cool shadows or vice versa), monochrome or duotone, pastel, earthy and warm, cool and clinical, sepia, cross-process.

**White balance**: warm or cool, neutral, deliberately pushed (tungsten orange, fluorescent green, shade blue).

**Tonal range**: full range, compressed or flat, high contrast, soft contrast.

**Always extract a hex palette.** Pull the dominant tones, the accent, and the shadow and highlight tints. The palette plus the grade description is what reproduces the color feel.

## 7. Texture and material

Skin: matte, dewy, glossy, textured and pore-level real, retouched smooth, airbrushed.

Fabric: matte cotton, sheen of silk or satin, knit, denim, leather, sequin or metallic, sheer or translucent.

Surfaces: brushed metal, glass, ceramic, raw concrete, wood grain, paper fiber, plastic gloss.

Note the grain or smoothness of the whole image as a layer on top of these.

## 8. Post-processing and film character

**Grain and noise**: clean and digital, fine grain, heavy grain, luminance vs color noise.

**Film emulations** (name one if it fits): Kodak Portra (warm, soft skin), Kodak Gold (nostalgic warm), Fuji Pro 400H (cool, pastel), Cinestill 800T (tungsten, red halation around highlights), Ilford HP5 (classic black and white grain), Polaroid (low contrast, vignette, color cast).

**Finishing**: vignette, halation or bloom around highlights, glow or diffusion (Pro-Mist look), heavy retouching vs natural, high clarity or texture, deliberate artifacts (dust, scratches, light leaks, date stamp).

## 9. Mood vocabulary

Premium and restrained, intimate and warm, cold and clinical, dreamy and ethereal, bold and graphic, nostalgic, tense or dramatic, playful, serene, editorial and aspirational, raw and candid, surreal.

Tie the mood back to the techniques that produce it, since that is the reproducible part.

## 10. Common editorial and creator looks

A few composite looks worth recognizing on sight, with the ingredients that define them:

- **High-fashion editorial**: long-lens compression, restrained desaturated grade, controlled studio or hard directional light, strong negative space, premium styling.
- **Clean beauty**: clamshell or ring light, even tones, dewy skin, minimal background, shallow falloff.
- **Cinematic moody**: low-key lighting, teal and orange or lifted matte blacks, anamorphic flares, shallow depth of field.
- **Nostalgic film**: warm or pastel emulation, visible grain, soft contrast, mild vignette, slightly imperfect framing.
- **Premium product**: controlled gradient light, deliberate reflections and shadows, seamless or textured surface, crisp focus, generous negative space.
- **Paper-cut or layered**: flat lighting, visible paper edges and drop shadows between layers, limited matte palette, tactile craft feel.
